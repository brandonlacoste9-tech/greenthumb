How often should you water houseplants?

There is no universal “water every Sunday” rule. Good schedules start with species needs, then adjust for light, pot size, humidity, and season. GardenGreen tracks intervals and can factor local weather when your garden plants are outdoors or climate-sensitive.

A simple schedule framework

  1. Start with the species interval (for example every 7 days for many tropicals, 14+ for succulents).
  2. Check soil before watering—finger test or moisture meter on larger pots.
  3. Water thoroughly until excess drains, then empty the saucer.
  4. Adjust seasonally—most plants need less in winter, more in bright summer growth.

Weather-aware watering

Heat and dry air speed evaporation; cool and humid conditions slow it down. Outdoor plants may skip a watering after rain. In GardenGreen, garden tasks and weather context help you prioritize what actually needs water today.

FAQ

Is it better to water on a fixed day?

A reminder day is fine as a prompt, but always confirm soil moisture. Fixed days without checking are a common path to root rot.

How much water should I give?

Water until it runs from drainage holes for most potting mixes. Avoid frequent tiny sips that leave the bottom dry or salt-loaded.

Do plants need less water in winter?

Usually yes. Lower light and slower growth mean soil stays wet longer. Extend intervals and check before every watering.
