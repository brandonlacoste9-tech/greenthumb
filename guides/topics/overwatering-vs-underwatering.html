Troubleshooting

Overwatering vs underwatering

Both over- and underwatering can cause yellow leaves and droop—so look at soil and stems, not leaves alone.

Side-by-side symptoms

Likely overwatering

  • Soil wet for many days
  • Soft, yellow leaves
  • Mushy stems or foul soil smell
  • Fungus gnats

Likely underwatering

  • Soil dry and pulling from pot edges
  • Crispy brown edges
  • Lightweight pot
  • Dramatic droop that firms up after a thorough water

Recovery steps

If overwatered: stop watering, increase airflow and light, check drainage holes, and repot into fresh mix if roots are brown and mushy.

If underwatered: soak thoroughly until water drains, let excess leave the saucer, and resume a check-then-water routine rather than tiny daily sips.

Match the plant to the routine

Succulents and ZZ plants hate wet feet. Ferns and peace lilies prefer more even moisture. Read the species guide before copying a neighbor’s schedule.

FAQ

Can a plant be both over- and underwatered?

Yes over time—chronic shallow watering can leave lower roots dry while the top looks wet, or compacted soil can hold water poorly. Full soaks and airy soil help.

Should I use ice cubes to water?

Usually no. Cold shock is unhelpful for tropicals. Use room-temperature water and water the soil thoroughly instead.
