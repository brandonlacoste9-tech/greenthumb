Troubleshooting

Why are my plant leaves turning yellow?

Yellow leaves are one of the most common plant problems. The good news: patterns usually point to a cause. Use the checklist below before you repot or fertilize in a panic.

Quick diagnosis checklist

  1. Feel the soil. Soggy for days? Likely overwatering. Bone dry and crispy? Underwatering or heat stress.
  2. Look at which leaves yellow first. Older bottom leaves often age out or signal nitrogen/light issues. New growth yellowing can mean root or watering stress.
  3. Check light. Sudden move to a darker room can yellow leaves. Harsh direct sun can bleach or scorch.
  4. Inspect pests. Sticky residue, webbing, or speckles can mean insects stressing the plant.

Common causes

  • Overwatering / root rot — yellow, soft leaves; soil stays wet; pot may smell sour.
  • Underwatering — yellow then crispy edges; light pot; soil pulls from sides.
  • Low light — pale yellow, stretchy growth, few new leaves.
  • Nutrient deficiency — gradual yellowing with otherwise healthy roots (after checking water/light).
  • Natural aging — occasional lower leaf yellowing on otherwise thriving plants is normal.

What to do this week

  • Only water when the top of the soil is dry (depth depends on species).
  • Empty saucers so roots are not sitting in water.
  • Move to brighter indirect light if the plant is in a dark corner.
  • Hold fertilizer until you see healthy new growth again.

FAQ

Should I cut off yellow leaves?

Yes, once a leaf is mostly yellow it will not turn green again. Remove it cleanly to tidy the plant and reduce pest hiding spots.

Can yellow leaves mean too much sun?

Yes. Bleached, washed-out yellow patches or crispy burn often mean excess direct sun. Move back to bright indirect light.

Why is my monstera getting yellow leaves?

Monsteras often yellow from overwatering or low light. Let the top third of soil dry, ensure drainage, and give bright indirect light.
